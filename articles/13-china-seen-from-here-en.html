China, seen from here

On 23 October I'm flying to China with two friends. Before seeing it for myself, I wanted to lay out what the numbers say, and where they fall short. The result is a seven-minute film rather than an article.

    Why a film

    Since Covid, I've been reading a lot about China, especially its digital world. I had hunches, but I'd never really dug into the numbers. So before leaving, I asked myself four questions: their history, their talent, the digital wall between them and us, and my own trade, IT services.

    The film follows the same method as every Miradouro piece: an observation, sourced data, its limits, then questions. Every figure links to its source below.

    02

    What the numbers don't say

    • Chinese statistics are official: scopes set by the state, methods rarely published. Some series have been heavily revised (robot density, for instance).
    • Several figures come from research firms or the press (payment shares, OpenRouter traffic, iPhones in India): they are estimates, and two OpenRouter readings disagree on the starting point.
    • Wages are averages for the public sector and large organisations, converted at October 2026 exchange rates: the 2010 figure of $9,600 depends on the rate used (between $9,500 and $9,700). The comparison with France and Portugal is a Miradouro calculation, with different years.
    • Youth unemployment isn't comparable across countries: China excludes students, Eurostat includes them.
    • AI model prices are compared per token; per completed task, the gap often shrinks.
    • And I haven't been to China yet. This film is a starting point, not a verdict.

    Their history
    • China's share of world output in 1820 (about a third) and around 1950 (about 5%), purchasing-power estimates. Maddison, Contours of the World Economy, 2007 (table) · L. J. Lau, CUHK, 2026
    • About a fifth of the world economy today, in purchasing power (19.9% in 2026, projection). IMF, World Economic Outlook
    Talent & tech

    • PISA 2018: Beijing, Shanghai, Jiangsu and Zhejiang (not all of China) first in all three subjects; China did not take part in 2022. OECD, PISA 2018 Results, volume I
    • 57% of elite AI researchers studied in China; more of them now work in China than in the US (NeurIPS 2025 cohort). Carnegie Endowment, 23/09/2026 · The Next Web
    • Open models five to ten times cheaper per token than the best US model. Digital in Asia, price tracker, 25/07/2026
    • Over half of developer traffic on OpenRouter going to Chinese models, week of 14 September 2026. CNBC, 26/09/2026 · Trending Topics, March 2026 (another reading)
    • WeChat: over 1.4 billion monthly users. Tencent, Q1 2026 results
    • Payment apps: 89% of in-store purchase value in China in 2025. Global Payments Report 2026 via Caixin, 20/05/2026
    • Facial-recognition payment (“Smile to Pay”) in shops since 2017. CNBC, 04/09/2017
    • Industrial robots: almost 3 installations in 5 worldwide went to China (2025). IFR, 24/09/2026
